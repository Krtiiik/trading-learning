A trend is the general direction a price moves in over a stretch of time, often weeks or months. It is what you see on a [[price-chart]] when you look past the day-to-day zigzags.

## Up, down or sideways

- **Uptrend**: the price moves up overall.
- **Downtrend**: the price moves down overall.
- **Sideways** (no trend): the price moves back and forth between roughly the same levels.

## Reading it from highs and lows

A price rises to a high, falls back to a low, then rises again. These turning points are [[peak-and-trough|peaks and troughs]].

Compare these highs and lows:

- **Higher highs and higher lows**: uptrend.
- **Lower highs and lower lows**: downtrend.
- **Neither, or a mix**: no clear trend; often the price moves sideways.

The trend lasts as long as this pattern holds. In an uptrend, a lower high is often an early sign of trouble, and a first lower low warns that the trend may be over.

## Example

A [[stock]] moves like this over three months:

| Turning point | Price |
|---------------|-------|
| Low           | $40   |
| High          | $48   |
| Low           | $44   |
| High          | $53   |
| Low           | $49   |
| High          | $58   |

Each high ($48, $53, $58) and each low ($40, $44, $49) is above the one before. That is an uptrend, even though the price fell twice on the way. If the next dip reaches $47, below the last low of $49, the uptrend is in doubt.

## Seeing it with a moving average

A [[moving-average]] smooths out the zigzags, so the direction is easier to see. With a 50-day simple average (the average of the last 50 daily closing prices) on a daily chart:

- Price mostly above a rising average: uptrend.
- Price mostly below a falling average: downtrend.
- Price crossing back and forth over a flat average: no clear trend.

## It depends on the timeframe

The same stock can be in an uptrend on a weekly chart and a downtrend on an hourly chart at the same time; the [[timeframe]] (how much time each candle covers) changes what you see. A few bad hours can be just a short dip inside a rise that lasts months. So say which chart and which stretch of time you mean: "up on the daily chart over the last six months" is clearer than just "up".

## Why it matters

Many traders prefer to trade with the trend: buy in an uptrend, and stay out or sell in a downtrend. [[trend-following|Trend following]] is a [[strategy]] (fixed rules for when to buy and sell) built entirely on that idea. Strategies such as [[mean-reversion]], which bets that a price will come back to its usual level, work better when there is no clear trend.

## Watch out

You can only see a trend after it has started, and you only know it has ended after it has turned. A chart or average shows only what the price has done so far, not where it goes next.
