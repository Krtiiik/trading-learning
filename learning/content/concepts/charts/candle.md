A candle (or candlestick) is one bar on a [[price-chart]]. It sums up everything the price did during one period, such as one day or one hour. That period is the chart's [[timeframe]].

## The four prices

Each candle shows four prices, together called [[ohlc]]:

- **Open**: the price at the start of the period.
- **High**: the highest price reached.
- **Low**: the lowest price reached.
- **Close**: the price at the end of the period.

## Reading the shape

- The thick part is the **body**. It spans from the open to the close.
- The thin lines above and below are the [[wick|wicks]]. They reach up to the high and down to the low.
- The colour tells the direction. A **green** (or hollow) candle closed higher than it opened: the price went up. A **red** (or filled) candle closed lower: the price went down.

## Example

A stock opens the day at $100, climbs to $108, drops to $97 and closes at $105. The daily candle is green, its body runs from $100 to $105, the upper wick reaches $108 and the lower wick reaches $97.

## Why candles instead of a line

A line chart joins only the closing prices. A candle also shows how far the price swung and who won the period, buyers or sellers. See [[line-vs-candle-chart]].

Small groups of candles often form recognisable shapes, called [[candlestick-pattern|candlestick patterns]].
