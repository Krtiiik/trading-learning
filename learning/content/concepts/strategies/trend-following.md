Trend following is a [[strategy]] built on one idea: prices that have started moving in a direction often keep moving that way for a while. So you buy when a price is rising, sell (or stay out) when it is falling, and stay in for as long as the [[trend]] lasts.

You do not try to predict where the trend starts or ends. You wait until it is visible, ride it, and leave once it is clearly over.

## A simple version

Many trend-following systems are built from a [[moving-average]]:

- **Enter** when the price closes above its 100-day moving average.
- **Exit** when the price closes below it.

Those are the [[entry-and-exit-rules]]. Adding a [[stop-loss]] limits how much a single trade can lose.

## What it feels like

Most trades are small losses: the price crosses the average, then turns back. A few trades catch a long trend and make large gains. The few big winners pay for the many small losers, so the [[win-rate-and-expectancy|win rate]] is often below 50% while the strategy still makes money overall.

## Example

A stock trades around $40 for months, then starts to climb. It closes above its 100-day average at $42, and you buy. It rises for half a year to $70, with dips along the way, and finally closes below the average at $64. You sell: a gain of $22 per share, even though you missed both the bottom and the top.

## Watch out

In a sideways market, trend following loses again and again on false starts. It needs patience through those stretches, and a [[backtest]] can show how long and deep they were in the past.

The opposite approach, betting that prices return to their usual level, is [[mean-reversion]].
