This lesson walks through a chart the way you would read one for the first time. You need only seven ideas, listed above. Each one has its own short page if you want to look it up.

## Step 1: What does one candle cover?

Open a [[price-chart]] and first check its [[timeframe]]. On a daily chart, each [[candle]] is one trading day. On an hourly chart, each is one hour. The timeframe changes how the same prices look, so always know which one you are looking at.

## Step 2: What happened in one period?

Pick the last candle and read its four prices, the [[ohlc]]: open, high, low and close. Then look at its shape:

1. Its colour tells whether the day closed up (usually green) or down (usually red).
2. Its body shows the move from open to close.
3. Its [[wick|wicks]] show how far the price swung beyond that.

A long body with short wicks means the price moved one way all day. A small body with long wicks means it swung a lot but ended near where it started: buyers and sellers were undecided.

## Step 3: Which way is it going overall?

Zoom out and look at the last few months instead of a single candle. Compare the highs and lows to find the [[trend]]: up, down or sideways.

Then add a 50-day [[moving-average]] to the chart. Check two things: is the price mostly above or below the average, and is the average rising, falling or flat? [[trend|The trend page]] shows what each answer means.

## Try it

Open any daily chart of a large [[stock]] and answer:

1. Did the last candle close up or down, and was the move big or small compared with the candles before it?
2. Over the last three months, are the highs and lows rising, falling, or flat?
3. Is the price above or below its 50-day moving average, and which way is the average pointing?

If your answers to 2 and 3 agree, you have read the trend. That is the starting point for strategies such as [[trend-following]].
