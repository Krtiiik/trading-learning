Trading psychology is how feelings such as fear, hope and pride change what you do during a trade. Many traders break good rules at the worst moments.

## Why losses push so hard

In Amos Tversky and Daniel Kahneman's 1992 experiments, a loss hurt about twice as much as an equal gain pleased. This is **loss aversion**. So a loss you have not yet sold feels easier to bear than one you take, and a gain feels safest once it is sold. That is why traders hold losers and sell winners early.

## The common mistakes

Take one trade: 25 shares bought at $42 with a [[stop-loss]] at $38, four [[atr|ATRs]] below, so a hit stop loses $100, 1% of a $10,000 account. A [[trend-following]] rule sells on a close below the 100-day [[moving-average]].

- **Selling winners early.** The price reaches $48, and you sell to "lock in" $150. Following the rule, you would have held and sold at $64, a $550 gain. Trend following loses most trades and lives on a few big [[win-rate-and-expectancy|wins]].
- **Holding losers.** The price falls; you ignore the sell signal and cancel the stop, hoping to get back to even. Together with selling winners early, this is the **disposition effect**. Terrance Odean studied 10,000 accounts at a US broker, 1987 to 1993: investors did this, and in the next year the winners they had sold did about 3.4% better than the losers they kept. They sold the wrong stocks.
- **Moving the stop.** You move it down to $35 "to give it room". A hit stop now loses $175, 1.75% of the account, not the 1% that [[position-sizing]] chose the 25 shares for.
- **Revenge trading.** After three losses, you take an unplanned or bigger trade to win the money back. Bigger losses often follow, and the [[drawdown]] can get deeper faster.
- **Overconfidence after wins.** A run of gains feels like skill, so you trade more and risk more. Brad Barber and Odean sorted 66,465 US households (1991 to 1996) by how much they traded. Before costs, the busiest fifth did about as well as the rest; after costs, it earned 11.4% a year, the average household 16.4%.

## Defences

Decide while you are calm:

- **Written rules.** Write your [[entry-and-exit-rules]] and the size before you buy.
- **Fixed risk per trade.** Risk the same 1% after wins and after losses.
- **Orders placed in advance.** Place the stop when you buy, and never cancel it or move it further away. A [[gap]] can still sell it lower.
- **A journal.** Record each trade and whether you followed the rules in a [[trading-journal]].

## Watch out

- **A win from a broken rule is still a broken rule.** It builds a habit that later costs money.
- **Paper trading (pretend money in a demo account) hides real fear.** Start live small, as in the lesson [[testing-a-trading-rule]].
