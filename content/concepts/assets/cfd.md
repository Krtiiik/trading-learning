A **CFD** (contract for difference) is a contract with a [[broker]] that pays the change in a price. You never own the asset; the broker settles the price difference in cash. CFDs are common in the EU and UK but not offered to private traders in the US.

## How it works

CFDs cover [[stock|stocks]], [[index|indices]], [[forex-pair|forex]], [[commodity|commodities]] and [[cryptocurrency]]. You put down only a deposit (margin), so every CFD uses [[leverage]].

The broker is the other side of your contract and quotes its own prices; you close the CFD only with that broker, not on an [[exchange]].

## Example

You buy a CFD on 25 shares of a $42 stock: a position value of $1,050. For single stocks, the EU limit is 5:1, so the broker holds 1/5 as margin: $210.

| Price when you close | Change | Gain or loss | Part of your $210 |
|----------------------|--------|--------------|-------------------|
| $46 | +$4 | +$100 | +48% |
| $38 | −$4 | −$100 | −48% |

For a [[dividend]], the broker credits buyers a matching cash amount (often after tax) and charges sellers. A CZK or EUR account converts the dollar result ([[currency-risk]]).

## Going short

Selling a CFD first gains when the price falls: sell 25 at $42 with a buy stop at $46 (an order that buys back once the price reaches $46), as in [[short-selling]], risking $100. You borrow no shares, but many brokers add a borrow fee and can close the short if shares get hard to borrow. A [[gap]] to $50 loses $200.

## Costs

- **The [[spread]]** (the gap between the best price buyers offer and the best price sellers want). Brokers may charge commission on stock CFDs and quote a narrower spread.
- **[[fees-and-commissions|Overnight financing]]**: interest for each night held, on the full $1,050, not on your $210. Sellers usually pay less or get a small credit.

## EU protections

ESMA, the EU markets regulator, first set these rules in 2018; now each country's regulator applies them as its own rules, in Czechia the ČNB (the Czech National Bank). The UK regulator, the FCA, has almost the same rules but bans crypto CFDs:

- **leverage caps** by asset, and a [[leverage|stop out]]: if your account value falls to half the required margin, the broker closes your positions;
- **negative balance protection**: you cannot lose more than your account;
- **a warning**, in the broker's ads and website, of the share of its private traders' accounts that lose money, often 70% or more.

The broker must also ask about your knowledge and experience (the **appropriateness test**). If CFDs do not suit you, it warns you but usually lets you trade.

## Watch out

- **"Professional client" offers** bring more leverage. You need two of: about 10 large trades a quarter over the last year, over €500,000 in cash and investments (not your home), a year in a finance job with such trades. You lose the leverage caps and often negative balance protection.
- **Tax differs from shares.** In Czechia, shares held more than 3 years are tax-free, but CFD gains are taxed however long you hold. See [[taxes-on-trading]].
