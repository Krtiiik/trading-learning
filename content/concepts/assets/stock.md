A company can split its ownership into millions of equal pieces called **shares**. When you buy a stock, you buy some of those shares and own part of the company. A stock's price is the price of one share: "a stock trades at $50" means one share costs $50.

## What you get

- **A part of the company's value.** If the company grows and people think it is worth more, the share price tends to rise. If it struggles, the price tends to fall.
- **Sometimes a share of the profit.** Some companies pay a [[dividend]]: money a company pays for each share you own. US companies usually pay every three months; many European companies pay once or twice a year. The company decides each payment and can cut or stop it. Many pay nothing and keep the profit.
- **A vote.** Shareholders can vote on big decisions, such as who sits on the board that oversees managers. Each share usually gives one vote, though some give none.

## How you buy one

Shares trade on an [[exchange]]: a marketplace where buyers and sellers are matched, such as the New York Stock Exchange or the Prague Stock Exchange. You open an account with a [[broker]], and the broker sends your order to an exchange, or another place where orders are matched, for you.

## The price you see

Each time a buyer and a seller agree on a price, that is one trade, and apps usually show the price of the last trade. Buying right now usually costs a little more than that (see [[bid-and-ask]]). A [[price-chart]] shows how the price changed over time.

## Example

A company has 1,000,000 shares and each one trades at $50. The whole company is then valued at $50,000,000 (price times number of shares). If you buy 10 shares for $500, you own 0.001% of it. If the price rises to $55, your shares are worth $550, a gain of $50 or 10%.

## Many stocks at once

Many people buy a basket of companies instead of picking single ones. An [[index]] is a number that tracks the combined price of such a basket. An [[etf]] is a fund that holds the basket, so you buy all of it in one trade.

## Watch out

- **A high share price does not mean a big company or a bad deal.** The company's value is price times shares. A company with 1,000,000 shares at $500 is worth $500 million; one with 1,000,000,000 shares at $5 is worth $5 billion. To judge a price, people compare the company's value with its profit: see [[valuation]].
- **A stock can fall to zero.** If the company fails, you can lose all you paid.
- **A US stock's result also depends on the dollar.** If your money is in CZK or EUR, the exchange rate changes your gain or loss (see [[currency-risk]]).
