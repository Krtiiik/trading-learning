In the currency market (**forex**, short for foreign exchange), you trade one currency against another. A **forex pair** is the price of one currency in units of the other.

## Base and quote

A pair is written like **EUR/USD** (euro and US dollar).

- The first currency is the **base**. The price is for one unit of it.
- The second is the **quote**. The price is given in it.

So 1.0850 means 1 euro costs 1.0850 US dollars.

**Buying** a pair means buying the base and selling the quote: you expect the euro to rise. **Selling** it is the opposite.

The **majors** are the handful of most-traded pairs, such as EUR/USD, USD/JPY (Japanese yen) and GBP/USD (British pound). All include the US dollar and usually have the smallest [[spread|spreads]]. Not every dollar pair is a major: USD/MXN (Mexican peso) is an **exotic**, a less-traded pair with wider spreads.

## The pip

Traders count forex moves in **pips**. For most pairs, a pip is the 4th decimal place: 0.0001. For pairs with the yen, it is the 2nd: 0.01.

A pip's money value depends on your [[position-sizing|position size]] and is in the quote currency. 10,000 units of the base is called a **mini lot**; 100,000 is a **standard lot**.

## Example

You buy one mini lot (10,000 euros) of EUR/USD at 1.0850. Each pip is worth 10,000 × 0.0001 = **$1**. Ignoring the spread:

- The price rises to 1.0880: up 30 pips. You gain 30 × $1 = **$30**.
- The price falls to 1.0820: down 30 pips. You lose **$30**.

The spread (the gap between the best price buyers offer and the best price sellers want) is counted in pips too. A [[bid-and-ask|bid]] of 1.0849 and an ask of 1.0850 is a 1-pip spread. Buying, then selling, at the market price costs that pip: $1 on this position.

## Where you trade it

Most forex trades between banks and brokers, not on an [[exchange]]. In the EU and UK, most forex for private traders is traded as [[cfd|CFDs]] (contracts with a [[broker]] that pay the change in a price). They are not offered to private traders in the US, where brokers offer spot forex accounts instead. Either way, you gain or lose as if holding the currency.

## Watch out

- **[[leverage|Leverage]] is common in forex.** Brokers let you trade many times your money, multiplying gains and losses; in the EU and UK at most 30:1 for major pairs, 20:1 for others.
- **Your gain is in the quote currency** (dollars, for EUR/USD). In a CZK or EUR account, the broker converts it, often for a fee, and the rate moves too ([[currency-risk]]).
- **Many brokers show one more decimal**, a tenth of a pip: in 1.08505, the last 5 is half a pip.
- **Holding a position overnight brings an interest charge or credit** (a swap), set by both currencies' interest rates. With the broker's charge, it is often a cost. See [[fees-and-commissions]].
