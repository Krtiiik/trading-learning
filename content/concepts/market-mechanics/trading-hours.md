**Trading hours** are the times when you can trade in a market. A stock [[exchange]] sets its own hours and gives them in its local time. This page adds CET (Central European Time; CEST in summer).

## The regular session

The **regular session** is an exchange's main trading day. A stock's daily [[ohlc|open and close]] come from it.

| Exchange | Regular session (local time) |
|----------|------------------------------|
| New York Stock Exchange, Nasdaq | 9:30–16:00 New York time (15:30–22:00 CET) |
| London Stock Exchange | 8:00–16:30 UK time (9:00–17:30 CET) |
| Xetra (Frankfurt) | 9:00–17:30 CET |
| Prague Stock Exchange | about 9:00–16:15 CET (closing auction from 16:10) |
| Tokyo Stock Exchange | 9:00–15:30 Japan time (1:00–7:30 CET; 2:00–8:30 in summer time), closed 11:30–12:30 for lunch |

Exchanges close at weekends and on most public holidays.

## Opening and closing auctions

Most big stock exchanges start and end the regular session with an **auction**. For some minutes, orders collect but do not trade. Then the exchange fills them at the one price at which the most shares can trade. The closing auction sets the official close.

## Pre-market and after-hours

In the US, you can also trade a [[stock]] outside the regular session: **pre-market** usually from 4:00, and **after-hours** usually until 20:00 New York time (10:00 to 2:00 CET). Together these are **extended hours**. Some brokers also offer an **overnight** session. Your [[broker]] decides which sessions you can use. EU brokers often send orders for US stocks to German markets (Tradegate, gettex, LS Exchange), roughly 7:30 to 22:00 or 23:00 CET, with wider spreads outside US hours.

Extended hours have risks:

- **Thin order book.** Few orders wait, so [[liquidity]] is low.
- **Wide [[spread]].** The best buy and sell prices ([[bid-and-ask|bid and ask]]) are often far apart.
- **Limit orders only.** Many US brokers then accept only limit orders, which trade at your price or better (see [[order-types]]). A stop order (sell at the market once the price reaches your level) usually works only in the regular session, so your [[stop-loss]] may not protect you outside it.

## Example

A US stock closes at $40 at 16:00. At 18:00, the company reports bad earnings (its quarterly profit). After hours, the bid is, for example, $35.80 and the ask $36.40, with only a few hundred shares at each. A market sell for 1,000 shares, if your broker allowed one, would get $35.80 for the first few hundred and less for the rest.

Next morning, the opening auction sets the price at $35. The daily chart usually leaves out extended-hours trades, so it shows a [[gap]] down from $40 to $35.

## Forex and crypto

- **[[forex-pair|Forex]]** trades 24 hours a day on weekdays, from Sunday evening to Friday evening New York time (about 23:00 Sunday to 23:00 Friday CET). It is busiest when London and New York are both open.
- **[[cryptocurrency|Crypto]]** trades all day, every day, but fewer orders wait at quiet hours and at weekends.

## Watch out

- **Convert the time zone.** "Opens at 9:30" means New York time: 15:30 in Prague or Berlin, most of the year.
- **Clocks change on different dates.** The US and Europe change to summer time in different weeks, so for some weeks New York is 5 hours behind Prague, not 6.
- **The first minutes are jumpy.** Just after the open, prices jump and spreads are often wider.
