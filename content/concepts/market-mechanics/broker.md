A **broker** sends your orders to a market and holds your account: you cannot send orders to an [[exchange]] yourself.

## What a broker does

- **It holds your account.** It keeps your cash and the assets you buy (**custody**). Shares are often held in the broker's name, but its records name you as the owner.
- **It passes on your orders.** It sends your [[order-types|orders]] to an exchange or another market, or fills (carries out) them itself.
- **It keeps the records**: statements and often a yearly tax statement (see [[taxes-on-trading]]).
- **It can lend you money**, in a **margin account**. See [[leverage]].

## Two kinds of broker

- **A broker that passes your order on** to a market. Your gain or loss does not change its income.
- **A broker that takes the other side.** It sells when you buy, and buys when you sell. A **market maker** (a large trading firm that always offers to buy and sell) does this. So do most providers of **[[cfd|CFDs]]**: contracts with a broker that pay the change in a price. CFDs are common in the EU and UK but not offered to private traders in the US. Many [[forex-pair|forex]] brokers work this way. Some pass the risk on to another firm; others keep it, so your loss is their gain.

## Protection if a broker fails

Regulators license brokers and make them keep clients' assets apart. If assets are missing after a failure, a compensation scheme pays, up to a limit:

- **US:** SIPC, up to $500,000 per client, of which at most $250,000 in cash; not forex accounts.
- **UK:** FSCS, up to £85,000 per person per failed firm.
- **EU:** each country's scheme, at least €20,000 per person; some pay only 90% of the loss. Czechia's investor compensation fund, the Garanční fond obchodníků s cennými papíry, pays 90%, up to the CZK value of €20,000. An EU broker based in another country (such as Germany or Ireland) is covered by its home scheme, not the Czech one.

## Example

You hold 25 shares bought at $42 with a US broker that fails.

- Your shares were kept apart, so they usually move to another broker; SIPC replaces missing ones.
- No scheme covers price falls: if the price also drops to $38, you still lose 25 × $4 = $100.

## How to check a broker

Look the broker up in its regulator's list (in the US, FINRA BrokerCheck; in the UK, the FCA register; in the EU, ESMA's registers) and check that the website and contact details match. In Czechia, use the register of the ČNB (the Czech National Bank): a firm not in it may not offer you investment services in Czechia, even with a licence elsewhere.

## Watch out

- **[[cryptocurrency|Crypto]] may not be protected.** Most schemes do not cover coins held by a crypto exchange.
- **[[cfd|CFDs]] are risky.** CFD providers must show what share of private traders' accounts lose money: often 70% or more.
- **Fake brokers exist.** Promises of sure gains are a warning sign.
