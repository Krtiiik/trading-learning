**Leverage** means holding a position that is worth more than your own money. With [[stock|stocks]], your [[broker]] lends you the rest. With [[forex-pair|forex]] and CFDs (contracts with a broker that pay the change in a price), you put down only a deposit. Gains and losses come from the full [[position-sizing|position value]], so they are large next to your money.

## Leverage and margin

- **Leverage ratio** = position value / your own money.
- **Margin** is the part of your money the broker holds as a deposit while the position is open.
- A **margin account** is an account that allows leverage: a stock broker lends you cash for more shares, and a forex or CFD broker lets you hold positions larger than your deposit.

## Example

You have $1,000 and buy one mini lot (10,000 euros) of EUR/USD at 1.0850. Each pip (0.0001) is worth $1. The EU and UK limit for major forex pairs is 30:1, so the broker holds 1/30 of the position value as margin: $1,000 lets you hold at most about $30,000. You hold $10,850, which needs about $362 of margin. You use about 11:1.

| EUR/USD move | Price change | Gain or loss | Part of your $1,000 |
|--------------|--------------|--------------|---------------------|
| 30 pips | about 0.28% | $30 | 3% |
| about 108 pips | 1% | about $108 | about 11% |

A 1% price move changes your money by about 11%, up or down. Without leverage, $1,000 of euros gains or loses only $10.

## Margin call and stop out

Your **account value** is your $1,000 plus or minus the open gain or loss.

- **Margin call**: the account value falls below the required margin. Here, that is a fall of about 650 pips ($650). You must add money or close positions.
- **Stop out**: if the value keeps falling, the broker closes one or more positions itself, at the market price. In the EU and UK, a CFD broker must do this for private traders when the account value falls to half the required margin (about $170 here, as the margin shrinks with the price).

## Losing more than you deposit

A fast move or a [[gap]] (a jump in price, for example over a weekend) can go past the stop-out level. Then you can lose more than your account and owe the broker the difference. In the EU and UK, private CFD traders have **negative balance protection**: they cannot lose more than the money in their account.

## Limits by market

Regulators in many countries limit the leverage brokers give private traders. Brokers based elsewhere may offer much more, with less protection.

| Market | Asset | Maximum leverage |
|--------|-------|------------------|
| EU and UK, CFDs | Major forex pairs | 30:1 |
| EU and UK, CFDs | Other forex pairs, gold, major [[index|stock indices]] | 20:1 |
| EU and UK, CFDs | Other [[commodity|commodities]], other stock indices | 10:1 |
| EU and UK, CFDs | Single stocks | 5:1 |
| EU, CFDs | [[cryptocurrency|Cryptocurrency]] | 2:1 (the UK bans crypto CFDs, [[futures]] and [[option|options]] for private traders) |
| US, forex | Major pairs | 50:1 |
| US, forex | Other pairs | 20:1 |
| US, stocks | Stocks | 2:1 at purchase (Regulation T) |

For US stocks, after the purchase your own money must stay at 25% of the position value or more (the **maintenance margin**, set by FINRA, the US broker regulator).

## Watch out

- **A leveraged position usually costs interest** for each night held. See [[fees-and-commissions]].
- **The maximum leverage is a limit, not a target.** Use position sizing and a [[stop-loss]] to keep the money at risk small.
- **Leverage makes a [[drawdown]] (the account's fall from its high) deeper.** After a loss of 50%, you need a gain of 100% to get back.
