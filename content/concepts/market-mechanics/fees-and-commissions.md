**Fees and commissions** are the trading costs you see listed as a separate charge. A **commission** is the fee your [[broker]] charges for each order. Other fees go to the broker, the [[exchange]], a regulator or the government.

The [[spread]] (the gap between the best price buyers offer and the best price sellers want) is a separate cost on top, hidden in the price.

## The kinds of costs

- **Broker commission.** A flat amount per order (for example $2.50), per share (1 cent), or a percentage of the order value (0.1%). Many brokers now charge no commission on [[stock|stocks]].
- **Regulator fees and transaction taxes.** In the US, a seller of stocks pays a tiny fee to the regulator, the SEC: usually a fraction of a cent per $100 sold. In the UK, a buyer of most UK company shares pays stamp duty, a tax of 0.5% of the price; France and Ireland, among others, have similar purchase taxes. Czechia has none.
- **Currency conversion.** When an asset is priced in a different currency from your account (for example, euros in a CZK account), the broker converts your money. It often takes 0.25% to 1.5%, on the purchase and again on the sale unless you keep the foreign currency. The exchange-rate move itself is a separate risk ([[currency-risk]]).
- **Overnight financing.** With borrowed money ([[leverage]]) or [[cfd|CFDs]] (contracts with a broker that pay the change in a price), you usually pay interest (in forex, a swap) for each night you hold it.
- **Borrow fee.** Selling [[short-selling|short]] costs interest on borrowed shares.
- **Fund costs.** An [[etf]] takes a yearly fee from its assets, the expense ratio.
- **Account fees.** A monthly or inactivity fee.

Tax on gains is different: it is charged on profit, not on each order, and differs by country (see [[taxes-on-trading]]).

## Example

You buy 25 shares at $42 ($1,050) and sell them later at $64 ($1,600): a gain of $550. One round trip (buying, then selling) has two orders.

| Commission | Cost of round trip | Gain after commission |
|------------|--------------------|-----------------------|
| $2.50 per order | $5.00 | $545.00 |
| 0.1% of the order value | $1.05 + $1.60 = $2.65 | $547.35 |

Small here, fees grow large on:

- **Small orders.** A round trip of 2 shares at $42 ($84) also costs $5: about 6%.
- **Many trades.** 200 round trips in a year at $5 each cost $1,000. So trading on a short [[timeframe]] (candles of minutes, not days) costs more.

## How zero-commission brokers are paid

Some brokers, mostly [[forex-pair|forex]] ones, quote a wider spread than the market and keep the difference. Others earn on currency conversion, interest on your cash, or account fees. Many US brokers also get **payment for order flow**: large trading firms pay the broker for your orders and trade with you, earning on the spread. The UK bans it, and the EU bans it too (fully since July 2026).

## Watch out

- **"No commission" is not "no cost".** Check for conversion, financing and inactivity fees.
- **Add costs to every test.** A [[backtest]] without them looks better than real trading.
