**Fees and commissions** are the trading costs you see listed as a separate charge. A **commission** is the fee your [[broker]] charges for each order. Other fees go to the broker, the [[exchange]], a regulator or the government.

The [[spread]] (the gap between the best price buyers offer and the best price sellers want) is a separate cost on top, hidden in the price.

## The kinds of costs

- **Broker commission.** A flat amount per order (for example $2.50), an amount per share (for example 1 cent), or a percentage of the order value (for example 0.1%). Many brokers now charge no commission on [[stock|stocks]].
- **Regulator fees and transaction taxes.** In the US, a seller of stocks pays a tiny fee to the regulator, the SEC: usually a fraction of a cent per $100 sold. The rate changes at least once a year. In the UK, a buyer of most UK company shares pays stamp duty, a tax of 0.5% of the price; France and Ireland, among others, have similar purchase taxes. The broker passes these fees on to you.
- **Currency conversion.** When a stock is priced in a different currency from your account, the broker converts your money. It often takes 0.25% to 1.5%, on the purchase and again on the sale unless you keep the foreign currency in your account.
- **Overnight financing.** With borrowed money ([[leverage]]), you usually pay interest (in forex, a swap) for each night you hold the position.
- **Borrow fee.** Selling [[short-selling|short]] costs interest on the borrowed shares.
- **Fund costs.** An [[etf]] takes a yearly fee from its assets, the expense ratio.
- **Account fees.** A monthly or inactivity fee.

Tax on your gains is different: it is charged on your profit, not on each order, and it differs by country.

## Example

You buy 25 shares at $42 ($1,050) and sell them later at $64 ($1,600): a gain of $550. One round trip (buying, then selling) has two orders.

| Commission | Cost of round trip | Gain after commission |
|------------|--------------------|-----------------------|
| $2.50 per order | $5.00 | $545.00 |
| 0.1% of the order value | $1.05 + $1.60 = $2.65 | $547.35 |

Here the fees are small. They grow large on:

- **Small orders.** A round trip of 2 shares at $42 ($84) also costs $5: about 6%.
- **Many trades.** 200 round trips in a year at $5 each cost $1,000. So trading on a short [[timeframe]] (candles of minutes, not days), which usually gives more round trips, costs more.

## How zero-commission brokers are paid

Some brokers, mostly [[forex-pair|forex]] ones, quote a wider spread than the market and keep the difference. Others earn on currency conversion, interest on your cash, or account fees. Many US brokers also get **payment for order flow**: large trading firms pay the broker to receive your orders, and trade with you directly, earning a little on the spread. The UK bans it, and the EU bans it too (fully since July 2026).

## Watch out

- **"No commission" is not "no cost".** Check for conversion, financing and inactivity fees.
- **Add costs to every test.** A [[backtest]] without fees and the spread looks better than real trading.
