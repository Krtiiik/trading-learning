Volume is how much of an asset was traded during one period, such as one day or one hour. For a [[stock]], it is the number of shares traded; for other assets, units, contracts or coins.

## How it is counted

Each trade (a buyer and a seller agreeing on a price) counts once: if a buyer gets 100 shares from a seller, volume grows by 100, not 200.

Like the open, high, low and close prices ([[ohlc]]), volume belongs to one period, the chart's [[timeframe]]. A day's volume is usually the sum of its hours' volumes (for stocks, apps may leave out trading outside the regular session).

## Volume bars

Most [[price-chart|price charts]] show volume as bars under the [[candle|candles]], one per candle. A taller bar means more was traded. The bar usually takes its candle's colour: green when the candle closed higher than it opened, red when lower. Some apps, especially in China and Japan, use the opposite colours. A green bar shows only that the price rose: every share traded had a buyer and a seller.

## Example

A stock's average daily volume over 20 days is 2.1 million shares.

| Day | Close | Volume |
|-----|-------|--------|
| Mon | $40.00 | 1.9 million |
| Tue | $40.00 | 2.2 million |
| Wed | $44.80 | 8.4 million |
| Thu | $44.60 | 4.0 million |

On Wednesday, the company reports a much higher profit than expected. The price rises 12% and 8.4 million shares trade: four times the average. On Thursday, volume falls to 4.0 million, still about twice the average.

## Volume and liquidity

High volume usually comes with many orders waiting near the [[bid-and-ask|bid and ask]], so you can trade without moving the price much. That is [[liquidity]]. Low volume often comes with a wider [[spread]]: a bigger gap between those two prices, part of which you pay on each trade.

## Reading a move with volume

Many traders trust a price move more when volume is high: much was traded at the new prices, not just a little. A [[breakout]] on high volume is trusted more than one on low volume. This is a rule of thumb, not a reliable signal: moves on high volume also turn back.

## Watch out

- **Forex has no central volume.** Currencies ([[forex-pair|forex]]) trade between many banks and brokers, and no single [[exchange]] counts them all. Forex charts usually show **tick volume**: the number of price changes in the period. It often moves with real trading, but does not count units. Crypto is similar: each exchange shows only its own volume.
- **Compare an asset with itself.** That shows if volume is unusual, as on Wednesday. 1 million shares a day is a lot for a small company and normal for a large one.
- **Volume changes with the time of day.** For stocks, it is usually highest near the open and close of the [[trading-hours|trading day]]; in forex, when London and New York are both open.
- **The newest bar is not finished.** Its volume grows until the period ends.
