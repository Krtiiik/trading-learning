**Taxes on trading** are what the state takes from your trading profits and income: the gain when you sell, each [[dividend]], and interest. This page is a simplified overview of the 2026 rules, not tax advice: rules differ by country and change often.

## Tax on the gain, not on each order

Most countries tax a gain only when it is **realised**: when you sell for more than you paid. A realised loss can usually reduce other gains of that year. Taxes on each order, such as UK stamp duty, are a separate cost (see [[fees-and-commissions]]).

## US, UK and Czechia

The EU has no common tax on gains: each country sets its own, so the Czechia column applies only to Czech taxpayers.

| | US (federal) | UK | Czechia |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gain, held a short time | Like wages: 10% to 37% (one year or less) | 18% or 24% | 15% (23% on high incomes) |
| Gain, held long | 0%, 15% or 20% (more than one year) | Same as short | Tax-free after 3 years |
| Tax-free allowance | None | £3,000 of gains, £500 of dividends | Sales of shares, funds and bonds up to 100,000 CZK a year |
| Dividends | Mostly 0%, 15% or 20% | 10.75% to 39.35% | 15%, US tax credited |
| Interest | Like wages | 20% to 45%, after a £1,000 allowance (£500 for higher earners, none for top earners) | 15% (23% on high incomes) |
| Extra | 3.8% more for high earners; most states add their own tax | | |
| Savings account with a tax break | IRA, 401(k) | ISA, up to £20,000 a year | DIP |

## The Czech rules

The 100,000 CZK limit counts **sale proceeds**, not gains. The 3-year test covers [[stock|shares]], [[etf|ETFs]] and [[bond|bonds]]. When you sell part of a holding, the oldest units count as sold first. [[cryptocurrency|Crypto]] has its own 3-year test (up to 40 million CZK of sales a year) and its own 100,000 CZK limit. Gains on [[cfd|CFDs]], [[futures]] and [[option|options]] are always taxed.

The US keeps back 15% of a US dividend (withholding tax) if you filed form W-8BEN. This counts against the Czech 15%, so usually nothing more is due.

The **DIP** (dlouhodobý investiční produkt) is a retirement investment account. Payments of up to 48,000 CZK a year, a limit shared with pension savings and life insurance, come off your taxable income. Take the money out before you are 60, or less than 10 years after you start, and you pay the saved tax back.

With taxable gains or foreign dividends, you usually must file a tax return (small amounts next to a salary can be an exception).

## Example

You buy 25 shares at $42 ($1,050) and sell them about eight months later at $64 ($1,600): a gain of $550.

- **US resident:** if your wages are taxed at 22%, you pay $121. Held more than a year: 15%, $82.50.
- **UK resident:** at $1.31 per pound, about £420 of gain, within the £3,000 allowance if you have no other gains: no tax.
- **Czech resident:** at 21 CZK per dollar, the sale brings 33,600 CZK. If your sales of shares, funds and bonds this year total 100,000 CZK or less, there is no tax. Otherwise, 15% of the 11,550 CZK gain: about 1,730 CZK. Held more than 3 years: no tax.

## Watch out

- **Your tax is in your own currency.** A Czech trader counts the gain in CZK, so a stronger dollar can give a taxed gain even when the dollar price did not move, and a weaker one can shrink it (see [[currency-risk]]).
- **Frequent trading rarely gets the holding-period break**, as in a [[momentum|momentum strategy]], which trades every month.
