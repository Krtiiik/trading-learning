Rebalancing means trading back to the mix of assets you chose, after prices have changed it. You sell some of what grew too large and buy what became too small.

## Target mix and drift

Your **target mix** (also called **asset allocation**) is the share of your money you want in each kind of asset. A common example is 60% [[stock|stocks]] and 40% [[bond|bonds]]: a 60/40 mix. Stocks usually [[volatility|swing more]], so more stocks means more risk.

Prices do not move together, so the mix changes by itself: **drift**.

In [[momentum]], the target mix is equal money in each stock.

## Example

You put $10,000 into a 60/40 mix: $6,000 in stocks and $4,000 in bonds.

Stocks rise 30%; bonds stay flat. Stocks are now $7,800, bonds $4,000, the total $11,800. Stocks are about 66% ($7,800 / $11,800).

60% of $11,800 is $7,080. You sell $720 of stocks for bonds. That leaves $7,080 in stocks and $4,720 in bonds: 60/40 again.

## When to rebalance

- **Calendar rebalancing**: on a fixed date, for example once a year.
- **Threshold rebalancing**: only when a part is a set distance from the target mix, for example 5 percentage points (stocks above 65% or below 55%). Stocks at 66% pass that line.

## With new money instead of selling

Put regular new money into the part that is too small. In the example, $7,800 of stocks is 60% of $13,000, so bonds need $5,200: $1,200 of new money gives 60/40 again with no sale.

## What it does

- **It keeps the mix, and so roughly the risk, that you chose.** Without it, a 60/40 mix usually drifts toward stocks, because they tend to rise more, and the next crash hits harder (see [[drawdown]]).
- **It sells what rose most and buys what fell behind.** A fixed rule means [[trading-psychology|fear and hope]] do not decide.

## Watch out

- **Each sale can cost money.** You pay [[fees-and-commissions|fees]] and the [[spread]]. A sale at a gain can also mean tax. New money avoids this, and so do tax-advantaged accounts such as the US IRA, where sales are not taxed then. In Czechia, the DIP (a retirement investment account) gives its tax break on what you pay in, not on sales. But a sale of shares, funds or bonds held more than 3 years is tax-free, and so are such sales that total no more than 100,000 CZK a year (see [[taxes-on-trading]]).
- **Do not rebalance too often.** Small drifts often reverse; trading on each one adds costs.
- **It does not promise a higher return.** In a long rise in stocks, it keeps selling the winner, so a mix you never rebalance can end higher.
- **It means buying what is falling.** In a crash, the rule buys more stocks while they drop, and they can keep falling.
