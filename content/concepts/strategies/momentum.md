Momentum is a [[strategy]] that ranks many assets by their recent rise, buys the top few, and ranks them again on a schedule. The idea: recent top risers often keep beating the rest for some months.

## Momentum versus trend following

[[trend-following|Trend following]] compares one asset with its own past: is this price rising? Momentum compares assets with each other: which rose the most? In a falling market, it still holds the strongest.

On [[indicator]] pages, "momentum" means how strong the recent moves of one price were, as [[rsi]] and [[macd]] show.

## The rules

1. **Pick the group**: many similar assets, such as the [[stock|stocks]] in the S&P 500 [[index]].
2. **Measure** each one's return (its price change in percent) over a look-back, often 12 months. Many versions skip the latest month, because its jump often turns back.
3. **Buy the top group**, for example the top 10%, with equal money in each.
4. **Hold and rank again**, often monthly. Sell what fell out of the top group; buy what came in.

## The evidence

In 1993, Narasimhan Jegadeesh and Sheridan Titman studied US stocks from 1965 to 1989. Stocks with the highest returns over the past 3 to 12 months did better over the next months than those with the lowest. Later studies found the same in other countries and assets. These are averages over decades, before costs, with long bad stretches.

## Example

You have $10,000 and follow 10 stocks. On 1 March you rank them by their return over the last 12 months, minus the latest one.

| Rank | Stock | Return |
|------|-------|--------|
| 1 | A | +48% |
| 2 | B | +35% |
| 3 | C | +22% |
| 4 | D | +15% |
| 5 | E | +9% |
| 6 | F | +4% |
| 7 | G | −2% |
| 8 | H | −8% |
| 9 | I | −15% |
| 10 | J | −30% |

You buy the top 2: $5,000 each of A and B.

On 1 April you rank again. A is still first, but C has passed B. A is now worth $5,400 and B $5,100. You sell B and buy $5,100 of C. To make A and C equal, you move $150 from A to C: $5,250 each. That step is [[rebalancing]]. One change a month means 12 round trips a year.

## Watch out

- **Costs add up.** Each change pays [[fees-and-commissions|fees]] and the [[spread]] on both orders. Add them to any [[backtest]].
- **Momentum crashes.** When a falling market turns up, the biggest fallers often rise fastest and past winners lag. Versions that also sell the past losers short, [[short-selling|selling borrowed shares]] and hoping to buy them back cheaper, lost about two thirds of their value from March to May 2009: a deep, fast [[drawdown]].
- **Taxes.** In the US, gains held one year or less are taxed higher; in Czechia, a monthly re-rank sells most holdings within months, so they miss the 3-year exemption, unless your sales stay under the yearly 100,000 CZK limit (see [[taxes-on-trading]]).
- **It needs many assets.** Two stocks from one industry are almost one bet (see [[diversification]]). Real versions rank hundreds of stocks, or [[etf|ETFs]] on whole markets.
