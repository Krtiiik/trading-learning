Breakout trading is a [[strategy]] (fixed rules for when to buy and sell) that enters a trade when the price breaks past a [[support-and-resistance|support or resistance]] level. Here a trade means one position: a buy and the later sale that closes it.

A clear move past such a level is a [[breakout]]. A price that moved sideways between two levels for months (a trading range) sometimes starts a long move the way it breaks out.

## The rules

- **Entry**: buy on a close above the resistance zone. Or place a buy stop order just above it: an order that becomes a market buy once the price reaches that level (see [[order-types]]). It gets in sooner, but in a fast move or a gap it can fill above your price.
- **Stop loss**: a [[stop-loss]] a little below the old resistance zone, so small dips near the level do not trigger it. If the price falls that far, treat the breakout as failed.
- **Exit**: a target, often the height of the range added to the resistance level. Or a trailing stop: a stop loss you raise by a fixed rule, for example $2 below the highest close.

## Example

A [[stock]] moved sideways between about $45 and $55 for months. Its resistance zone is $54.80 to $55.30. One day it closes at $56, on about twice its usual [[volume]] (shares traded that day). The [[entry-and-exit-rules]]:

- **Enter**: near that close, buy at $56.
- **Stop loss**: $54, a small buffer below the resistance zone ($54.80). The risk is $2 per share.
- **Size**: a $10,000 account risks 1%, $100. [[position-sizing|Position sizing]] gives $100 / $2 = 50 shares.
- **Target**: the range is $10 high ($55 − $45). Added to $55, the resistance level: $65.

| Case | Exit | Per share | 50 shares |
|------|------|-----------|-----------|
| Target hit | $65 | +$9 | +$450 |
| Breakout fails | $54 | −$2 | −$100 |

The reward is $9 and the risk $2: a [[risk-reward]] of 4.5 to 1.

## What it feels like

Expect many small losses and a few large wins, as in [[trend-following|trend following]]. Many breakouts fail, and a close stop is often hit even when the breakout later works. So the [[win-rate-and-expectancy|win rate]] (the share of trades that end in a gain) is often low. Each loss costs about one planned risk ($100 here); the few large winners pay for them.

It is the opposite of [[mean-reversion|mean reversion]], which buys near support and expects the price to stay in the range.

## When it works

- **After a long sideways period.** More traders watch a range that held for months.
- **After a squeeze.** When the [[bollinger-bands|Bollinger bands]] are much narrower than usual, a bigger move often follows, in either direction.
- **On high volume.** A close past the level on high volume is trusted more, but it is no guarantee.

## Watch out

- **Gaps can jump past the stop.** A [[gap]] down can sell well below $54.
- **Do not chase.** Bought at $60, the risk to the $54 stop is $6 and the reward to $65 only $5: less than 1 to 1.
