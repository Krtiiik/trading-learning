A line chart and a candle chart draw the same price history in two ways. A line chart puts one dot at each period's close (its last price) and joins the dots. A candle chart draws one [[candle]] per period from four prices: the first, highest, lowest and last price of the period, called open, high, low and close ([[ohlc]]).

## Same days, two pictures

A [[stock]] over four trading days:

| Day | Open   | High   | Low    | Close  |
|-----|--------|--------|--------|--------|
| Thu | $40.00 | $40.60 | $39.70 | $40.30 |
| Fri | $40.30 | $41.90 | $38.40 | $40.20 |
| Mon | $40.10 | $40.50 | $39.20 | $40.00 |
| Tue | $35.00 | $36.00 | $34.50 | $35.50 |

**The line** uses only the last column: $40.30, $40.20, $40.00, $35.50. It is almost flat for three days, then slopes down $4.50 to Tuesday.

**The candles** show more:

- **Friday** has a small body and long [[wick|wicks]] on both sides. The price swung $3.50, from $38.40 to $41.90, but closed only 10 cents below Thursday. On the line, Friday looks like a calm day.
- **Tuesday** opens at $35, far below Monday's close of $40. That jump is a [[gap]]. The candles show empty space between Monday's low ($39.20) and Tuesday's high ($36). The line draws a smooth slope across it, as if the price slid down during the night.
- **Tuesday's candle is an up candle** (green in most apps), because it closed above its own open. The day still ended $4.50 below Monday. A candle's colour compares the close with the open of the same period, not with the close before.

## What each is best for

**Line chart:**

- A long-term overview, such as ten years of daily closes, where single candles are too small to read.
- Comparing two prices on one chart, for example a stock and an [[index]]. Two lines stay readable; two sets of candles overlap.
- Seeing the [[trend]], because a line has less noise (small, random moves with no lasting direction).

**Candle chart:**

- Reading each period: how far the price swung, and if it closed near its high or its low.
- Seeing gaps and long wicks.
- Finding [[candlestick-pattern|candlestick patterns]] and choosing where to put a [[stop-loss]].

## Other chart types

A third way is the **bar chart** (or OHLC chart; not the [[volume]] bars (how much was traded) under a chart). Each period is a vertical line from the low to the high, with a short tick to the left at the open and to the right at the close. It holds the same data as a candle, with ticks in place of a body.

## Watch out

- **A line can hide large swings and gaps.** In the example, a stop loss at $38.50, below every close the line shows, triggers on Friday and sells at about that price. Before you place a stop loss, look at the candles to see how low the price really went.
- **Daily charts depend on when the app ends its day.** A [[cryptocurrency]] trades all day, every day, so each app picks a time, often midnight UTC (London winter time). Two apps can draw different daily lines, and different daily candles, for the same coin.
