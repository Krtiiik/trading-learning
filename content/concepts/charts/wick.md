A wick is a thin line above or below the body (the thick part) of a [[candle]]. It shows how far the price went past the open and close during the period, up to the high or down to the low. Some people call wicks "shadows".

## Where the wicks start and end

The body is the thick part of the candle. It spans from the open (the first price of the period) to the close (the last price). See [[ohlc]] for all four prices.

Which end of the body is the open depends on the direction. On an up candle (usually green), the price closed higher than it opened, so the close is at the top and the open at the bottom. On a down candle (usually red), it is the other way round. So:

- The **upper wick** starts at the higher of the open and close, and runs up to the high.
- The **lower wick** starts at the lower of the two, and runs down to the low.

## Example

A [[stock]] opens the day at $100, climbs to $108, drops to $97 and closes at $105, as on the [[candle]] page.

- The body runs from $100 to $105: $5 tall.
- The upper wick runs from $105 to $108: $3.
- The lower wick runs from $100 down to $97: $3.

The price swung $11 from low to high, but only $5 stayed in the body. The wicks show the other $6: distance the price travelled during the period but did not keep at the close.

## What long and short wicks suggest

- **Long upper wick**: the price rose during the period but fell back before the close. Traders often read this as buyers losing control to sellers.
- **Long lower wick**: the price fell during the period but came back up before the close. Traders read this the opposite way: sellers losing control to buyers.
- **Short wick or none**: the price did not go much past the body on that side. An up candle with no upper wick closed at its high; a down candle with no lower wick closed at its low.

"Long" is relative: compare a wick with the body and with the candles around it.

## Watch out

- **One wick alone is weak evidence.** A long upper wick does not mean the price will fall next. Check where it formed (for example, after a long rise) and what the next candles do.
- **Wick length depends on the [[timeframe]].** A wick that looks long on a 5-minute chart may cover only a few cents, and on the daily chart it can sit inside the body where you cannot see it.
- **Two wicks do not show which came first.** OHLC loses the order. In the example, you do not know if the price reached $108 or $97 first.
- **A wick can still grow until the period ends.** On the newest candle, the high and low are not final yet.
