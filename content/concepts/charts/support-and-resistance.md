Support is a price level where falls stopped more than once: buyers came in, and the price turned back up. Resistance is a level where rises stopped more than once: sellers came in, and the price turned back down. On a [[price-chart]], they are where several [[peak-and-trough|peaks or troughs]] line up at about the same price. Larger shapes built from these turns are [[chart-pattern|chart patterns]].

## Example

A [[stock]] moves sideways between about $45 and $55 for months, as in the [[mean-reversion]] example:

| Turning point | Price  |
|---------------|--------|
| Trough        | $45.20 |
| Peak          | $54.80 |
| Trough        | $44.70 |
| Peak          | $55.30 |
| Trough        | $45.40 |

Three troughs between $44.70 and $45.40 make a support level near $45. Two peaks between $54.80 and $55.30 make a resistance level near $55.

## A zone, not one exact price

The troughs are not at the same cent. Treat a level as a narrow zone, here about $44.70 to $45.40. A [[wick]] a little below the zone, with a close (the last price of the period) back inside it, is usually not a break. A close below the zone usually counts as one; [[breakout]] lists stricter rules.

## Why levels can work

- **Many traders watch the same prices.** Traders who expect a turn at $45 buy there, and their buying helps make it.
- **Orders wait at the level.** Buyers place [[order-types|limit orders]] (orders that buy only at a set price or better) near $45. Sellers place them near $55.
- **Round numbers.** Prices such as $50 or $100 are easy to remember, so orders collect there.

A level works only while enough traders act on it.

## Broken resistance often becomes support

Turning points from the [[trend]] page: $40 (low), $48 (high), $44, $53, $49, $58. The first rise stopped at $48. That is only one peak, but traders watch it. Later the price rose past it, and the next fall stopped at $49, just above it. A price that had stopped a rise now stopped a fall. Broken support often becomes resistance.

## Using levels

- **A stop just beyond the level.** You buy near support at $45.50. A [[stop-loss]] also sells on a wick, so leave room: a little more than one [[atr]] ($1) below the zone: $43.50. Below it, the support has failed.
- **A target at the next level.** Sell just below resistance, near $54.50: the rise may stop a little short of it. The risk is $2 and the reward $9: a [[risk-reward]] of 4.5 to 1.
- **Breakouts.** [[breakout-trading|Breakout trading]] enters on a clear move past a level.

## Watch out

- **You see levels clearly only afterwards.** A trough is known only after the price has turned away from it (see [[peak-and-trough]]).
- **Stops collect just beyond levels.** Many traders put their stops in the same place. When they trigger together, the fall can speed up, and stop orders can sell lower than planned: [[liquidity|slippage]].
- **Levels depend on the [[timeframe]].** More traders watch daily levels than 5-minute ones.
