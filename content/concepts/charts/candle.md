A candle (or candlestick) is one bar on a [[price-chart]]. It sums up what the price did during one period, such as one day or one hour. That period is the chart's [[timeframe]].

## The four prices

Each candle shows four prices, together called [[ohlc]]:

- **Open**: the price of the first trade in the period.
- **High**: the highest price of any trade in the period.
- **Low**: the lowest price of any trade in the period.
- **Close**: the price of the last trade in the period.

## Reading the shape

- The thick part is the **body**. It spans from the open to the close.
- The thin lines above and below are the [[wick|wicks]]. They reach up to the high and down to the low.
- The colour tells the direction. Green (or hollow) usually means the candle closed higher than it opened: the price went up. Red (or filled) means it closed lower: the price went down. Some apps, especially in China and Japan, use the opposite, so check yours.

## Example

A [[stock]] opens the day at $100, climbs to $108, drops to $97 and closes at $105. The daily candle is up (usually green), its body runs from $100 to $105, the upper wick reaches $108 and the lower wick reaches $97.

## Why candles instead of a line

A line chart joins only the closing prices. A candle also shows how far the price swung and who won the period, buyers or sellers. See [[line-vs-candle-chart]].

Small groups of candles often form recognisable shapes, called [[candlestick-pattern|candlestick patterns]].

## Watch out

- **A candle loses the order.** In the example, you do not know if the price reached $108 before or after $97.
- **The newest candle is not finished.** Until its period ends, its close is just the latest price, and the candle can still change colour.
