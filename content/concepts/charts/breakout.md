A breakout is a clear move of the price past a [[support-and-resistance|support or resistance]] level that had stopped it before. It can go up, through resistance, or down, through support.

## What counts as a breakout

A level is a narrow zone, not one exact price. Most traders count a breakout only when a period (one day, say) closes beyond the zone: its last price is past it. A [[wick]] that goes through, with a close back inside, does not count.

Some traders want more than a plain close beyond the zone:

- **A set distance.** For example, a close at least half an [[atr]] beyond the zone.
- **Two closes.** Two periods in a row close beyond the zone.

Stricter rules filter out more false breakouts, but they also confirm the real ones later, at a worse price.

## Confirmation by volume

[[volume|Volume]] is how much was traded in a period. A breakout on high volume is trusted more than one on low volume: many traders acted at the new prices. This is only a rule of thumb: studies of past charts find it helps only a little, and high-volume breakouts can fail too.

## Example

A [[stock]] moves sideways between about $45 and $55 for months. The resistance zone is $54.80 to $55.30, the support zone is $44.70 to $45.40, and the ATR is $1.

One day the stock closes at $56, above the resistance zone, on about twice its usual volume. The close is 70 cents beyond the zone, more than half an ATR: a breakout.

| Day   | What happens             | Close        | Reading           |
|-------|--------------------------|--------------|-------------------|
| Day 1 | Rise past resistance     | $56.00       | Breakout          |
| Later | Dip, low near $55.20     | above $55.30 | Retest that holds |
| Or    | Fall back below the zone | below $54.80 | False breakout    |

## The retest

After a breakout, the price often comes back to the old level. This is a **retest**. Say the stock later dips to about $55.20, inside the old resistance zone, and turns up again: the old resistance now acts as support. Many traders read this as a sign that the breakout is real.

## The false breakout

In a **false breakout**, the price breaks out and then comes back between the old levels. Say the stock closes below $54.80 a few days later. The breakout has failed, and traders who bought at $56 hold a loss.

## Downward breakouts

The same rules work the other way: a close below the support zone, under $44.70, is a downward breakout. Prices can fall without heavy trading, so many traders care less about volume on a downward break.

## Where breakouts come from

Breakouts often follow a calm time. A [[bollinger-bands|Bollinger band]] squeeze, when the bands are much narrower than usual, shows such a time, but not the direction of the break.

## Watch out

- **You know only afterwards.** On the day of the break, you cannot tell a real breakout from a false one.
- **A retest can turn into a false breakout.** If the dip closes below the zone, the level failed.
- **[[gap|Gaps]] can jump past a level.** The price can open far beyond the zone.
