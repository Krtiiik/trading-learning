A volume profile is an [[indicator]] that shows how much was traded at each price level over a time span you choose. It is drawn as horizontal bars next to the [[price-chart]]: the longer the bar, the more [[volume]] traded at that price. Volume bars under the [[candle|candles]] give one value per period; the profile gives one per price.

## Example

Take the stock from the [[support-and-resistance|support and resistance]] page (prices where falls or rises stopped more than once): it moves sideways between about $45 and $55 for months, a trading range. A profile over those months, with one bar for each $2 of price, could look like this:

| Price | Volume (million shares) |
|-------|-------------------------|
| Above $55 | 2 |
| $53 to $55 | 13 |
| $51 to $53 | 20 |
| $49 to $51 | 30 |
| $47 to $49 | 20 |
| $45 to $47 | 13 |
| Below $45 | 2 |

Of 100 million shares, 30 million traded between $49 and $51. Little traded above $55 or below $45, which the price only touched.

## The parts of a profile

- **Point of control (POC)**: the single price with the most volume, here near $50.
- **Value area**: the prices around the POC where most of the volume traded, usually about 70%. Apps start at the POC, then keep adding the busier of the two levels just above or just below, until they reach 70%. Here, $47 to $53 holds 20 + 30 + 20 = 70 million shares: the value area.
- **High-volume node**: a price area with long bars, where many shares traded. The area around $50 is one.
- **Low-volume node**: a dip of short bars between busier prices, which the price crossed quickly. The thin ends of a profile are low-volume too: here, the prices above $55 and below $45.

## How traders read it

- **High-volume nodes often act like support or resistance.** Many traders bought or sold there, and some act again when the price returns.
- **The price tends to move fast through low-volume prices.** If the stock closes above the resistance zone, as with the $56 close in [[breakout-trading]], little stands in its way until the next busy price, which a profile over a longer time span would show. Breakout trading sets its $65 target from the height of the trading range; the thin volume above $55 is one reason traders think the price can get that far.
- **The POC is a reference price.** When the price comes back into the value area, some traders expect it to drift toward the POC.

## Which time span

- A **fixed-range** profile covers the start and end dates you pick.
- A **session** profile makes a new profile for each [[trading-hours|trading day]].
- A **visible-range** profile covers the candles on your screen and changes when you scroll or zoom.

## Watch out

- **The profile depends on the time span you choose.** A profile over several months and one over a single week can show a different POC for the same stock.
- **It needs real volume data.** [[forex-pair|Forex]] has no central volume, so apps use tick volume (the number of price changes) instead.
- **A high-volume node is not a guarantee.** It shows where trading happened in the past, not where the price will stop next.
