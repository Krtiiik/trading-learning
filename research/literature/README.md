# Literature

Published evidence on crypto return patterns, collected by the Literature scout (`.claude/agents/literature-scout.md`) as a supply of hypotheses that do not come from our own data. One file per family; one line per pattern here.

A pattern listed here is a lead, not a finding: it becomes a hypothesis when a Researcher registers it. Each entry carries the sample end date of its source, because a paper whose sample runs past 2022 overlaps our dev or test segments.

## Time-series

Notes: [time-series.md](time-series.md).

- **Price above its 5- to 100-day moving average: long, otherwise cash.** Raises the Sharpe ratio over buy-and-hold on Bitcoin (1.92 to between 2.09 and 2.51) with break-even costs of 112 to 396 bps one way; the gain is in drawdown, not return, shrinks for long windows, and a later study finds only a one-week price-to-average ratio survives data-snooping control. Sources: Detzel et al. 2021 (sample to 2018-01), Hudson and Urquhart 2021 (to 2017-12, out-of-sample failure on Bitcoin in 2018), Wei et al. 2024 (to 2022-03). Testable here as published.
- **Short-horizon time-series momentum: the past week's return predicts the next one to four weeks.** Weekly coefficient 0.19 to 0.22 on Bitcoin, concentrated after very strong weeks; daily effect weak under bootstrap; weaker for Ethereum; no costs. Source: Liu and Tsyvinski 2021, working paper version (sample to 2018-05). Testable as a predictive claim; as a long-or-flat trading claim only in part.
- **Momentum lives at one to four weeks and is gone by eight (cross-sectional evidence, filed here for its horizon).** Winners-minus-losers earns 2.5 to 4.1 percent a week at 1- to 4-week lookbacks, nothing at 8 to 100 weeks; profit is in the long leg and in large coins. Source: Liu, Tsyvinski and Wu 2022, working paper version (sample to 2018-12). Not a time-series test.
- **Parent result: 12-month time-series momentum in futures.** Sign of the past 12-month return, held one month, long or short, volatility-scaled; alpha about 1.58 percent a month, gross. Source: Moskowitz, Ooi and Pedersen 2012 (sample to 2009-12, no crypto). Not testable here as published; its 12-month lookback has no support in crypto.
- **Unverified leads** (abstract only or not opened; see the notes): Grobys, Ahmed and Sapkota 2020 (20-day moving average on eleven coins, to 2018); Grobys and Sapkota 2019 (a null result on crypto momentum, to 2018); Tzouvanas et al. 2020; Han, Kang and Ryu (sample to 2023-08, overlaps dev: flagged); Deprez and Frömmel 2024 (sample dates unknown: flagged).

Added by the second run (2026-11-19, sections 4 to 7 of the notes). Fewer sources could be opened in full than in the first run; the numbered patterns rest mostly on arXiv papers, and the better-known journal results are leads.

- **Hourly and 10-minute Bitcoin returns are negatively autocorrelated (reversal).** Significant at lag 1 on hourly bars for Bitcoin; the two Ethereum pairs show the opposite sign; no sizes reported, no costs. Sources: Tartakovsky et al. 2020 (sample to 2019-07), Tang et al. 2024 (10-minute, to 2022-05). Testable here as a predictive claim on hourly bars; a trading claim faces a 24 to 50 bps round trip.
- **Daily returns show no stable autocorrelation of either sign (a null).** Nothing significant or consistent across four markets on daily bars; weekly lag 1 mostly positive. Source: Tartakovsky et al. 2020 (to 2019-07). Testable as published; a daily reversal hypothesis on large coins would go against the published direction.
- **Inverted leverage effect: volatility rises more after gains than after losses.** On 87 Binance coins the variance from positive returns drives next-day variance (coefficient 1.262 against -0.142) and volatility is lower after down days; on Bitcoin alone to 2019 the return-volatility correlation is positive but insignificant. Sources: Brini and Lenz 2024 (sample to 2022-10), Kunimoto and Kakamu 2021 (to 2019-08). Testable only in part: a claim about volatility, not returns.
- **Volatility is persistent and follows the clock, the precondition for volatility-managed exposure.** Log-volatility persistence 0.918 on daily Bitcoin; weekday terms improve volatility models out of sample. No openable source tests volatility-managed exposure or volatility-predicts-return on crypto. Sources: Kunimoto and Kakamu 2021 (to 2019-08), Hansen, Kim and Kimbrough 2021 (to 2021-07). Testable only in part, as our own extension (scale down only, no leverage).
- **Weekends are quiet: lower volatility and volume on Saturday and Sunday.** Three samples from 2013 to 2021 agree; none finds or tests a weekend effect in mean returns. Sources: Hansen, Kim and Kimbrough 2021 (to 2021-07), Kunimoto and Kakamu 2021 (to 2019-08), Petukhina, Reule and Härdle 2021 (two months of 2018). Testable here as a volatility or volume claim on daily or hourly bars.
- **Day of the week in returns: Friday in one study, Monday in another, not persistent in a third.** Bitcoin 2013 to 2019: Friday 0.415 percentage points above Wednesday, the only significant day; Monday not. Source: Kunimoto and Kakamu 2021 (to 2019-08); the Monday result and the null are leads. Testable as a predictive claim with a low prior; the weekday-to-bar mapping must be fixed first.
- **Hour of the day in returns: Bitcoin's best hours are the two before midnight UTC.** Holding 21:00 to 23:00 UTC earned 33 percent a year on Gemini with no costs, about 9 bps a trade. Source: Padyšák and Vojtko 2022, practitioner research (sample to 2022-02). Testable as a predictive claim on hourly bars; not viable as a trading claim after costs.
- **Volatility and volume spike at 00:00, 16:00 and (less) 08:00 UTC and in the first minute of each hour.** Linked by the authors to algorithmic trading and funding times; nothing on mean returns. Source: Hansen, Kim and Kimbrough 2021 (to 2021-07). Testable on hourly bars as a volatility or volume claim; the within-hour part is not testable.
- **A positive autocorrelation at lag 16 on hourly bars, on all four markets studied.** Unexplained and unreplicated. Source: Tartakovsky et al. 2020 (to 2019-07). Testable as a predictive claim; treat as a chance finding until a second source appears.
- **Unverified leads, second run** (abstract only or not opened; see the notes). Reversal: De Nicola 2021 (negative autocorrelation at 1, 2 and 4 hours, stronger after large moves), Caporale and Plastun 2019 (contrarian rule after overreaction days unprofitable: a null), Zaremba et al. 2021 and Bianchi, Babiak and Dickerson 2022 (daily reversal sits in small, illiquid, low-volume coins; the largest show daily momentum; to 2022-03), Fičura 2023 (sample dates unknown: flagged), Kitron and Wengrowicz 2026 (overlaps dev or test: flagged). Volatility: Baur and Dimpfl 2018, Moreira and Muir 2017 (no crypto), Lee and Wang 2025 (sample dates unknown: flagged). Calendar: Caporale and Plastun 2019 (Monday), Aharon and Qadan 2019, Baur et al. 2019 (return anomalies do not persist: a null), Miralles-Quirós 2022 (weekday by hour, to 2021-12). Intraday momentum and volume: Shen, Urquhart and Wang 2022 (first half-hour predicts last), Wen et al. 2022 (to 2020-05), Balcilar et al. 2017 and Bouri et al. 2019 (volume causality without a direction).

## Cross-sectional

Notes: [cross-sectional.md](cross-sectional.md). Nearly all sources sort 1,000 to 3,000 coins; our 40 sit in their top size and volume group, so results on large or liquid coins count most. Every source in the numbered entries ends by 2022-12.

- **Momentum at one to four weeks, in large and liquid coins.** Winners minus losers 2.5 to 4.1 percent a week, value-weighted; 4.2 percent among above-median coins against 0.6 below; +0.26 percent a day in the liquid third against -0.33 in the illiquid third; profit in the long leg. Failure: the factor is not significant over 2014 to 2020 (t = 0.95) after short-leg crashes in 2019 and 2020. Sources: Liu, Tsyvinski and Wu 2022, working paper (sample to 2018-12); Begušić and Kostanjčar 2019 (to 2019-01); Fičura 2023, abstract and introduction (to 2022-12); Dobrynskaya and Dubrovskiy 2022 (to 2020-11). Testable as a predictive claim and as a long-only trading claim.
- **A combined trend score from price and volume indicators.** Top minus bottom quintile 3.87 percent a week (t = 5.19), 3.40 among the largest 100 coins, 2.45 after 30 to 40 bps; profit in the long leg; turnover 68 percent a week. Source: Fieberg et al. 2025 (to 2022-05). Testable only in part: the published score needs market capitalisation and a re-reading; a simple composite is testable.
- **Daily reversal: yesterday's losers beat yesterday's winners.** 2.2 percent a day equal-weighted and 0.6 value-weighted among the 100 most liquid pairs, gross; on Binance alone 0.06 to 0.19 percent a day gross and negative after costs; absent or reversed in the largest coins. Sources: Bianchi, Babiak and Dickerson 2022, working paper (to 2022-03); Kakushadze 2018 (to 2018-08). Testable as a predictive claim with a low prior; a trading claim is not supported.
- **Reversal is stronger after abnormally low volume; a volume shock alone predicts little.** Low-volume reversal 1.22 percent a day against 0.54 after high volume (equal-weighted), 0.6 against -0.18 (value-weighted); the stand-alone low-minus-high volume-shock spread is zero after costs. Source: Bianchi, Babiak and Dickerson 2022 (to 2022-03). Testable as a predictive interaction; quote volume gives the shock exactly.
- **Size and price level: small and low-priced coins earn more.** Smallest minus largest quintile 3.4 percent a week, lowest minus highest price 3.9; the whole effect is the bottom quintile; size factor 61 percent a year over 2014 to 2020. Sources: Liu, Tsyvinski and Wu 2022 (to 2018-12); Dobrynskaya and Dubrovskiy 2022 (to 2020-11). Not testable as published (micro-caps, no market capitalisation); a rank by quote volume or price within the universe is a weak proxy with no published gradient.
- **Dollar volume and its volatility: low-volume coins earn more; turnover and Amihud illiquidity do not predict.** Lowest minus highest dollar-volume quintile 3.2 percent a week, volume volatility 3.0; volume in coins, volume over capitalisation and Amihud all insignificant. Sources: Liu, Tsyvinski and Wu 2022 (to 2018-12); Begušić and Kostanjčar 2019 (to 2019-01). Testable only in part: computable from quote volume, but the premium sits in the lowest-volume fifth of the market.
- **Distance from the one-week high.** A close near the past week's high predicts a higher next-week return in large, liquid coins (t = 4.93) and a lower one in small, illiquid coins (t = -9.03). Source: Fičura 2023, abstract and introduction only (to 2022-12; one unreplicated working paper). Testable as a predictive claim.
- **Low volatility in large coins.** Long-only portfolios of the 3 to 9 least volatile of 35 large coins earn 4.4 to 9.7 percent a month after costs with 6- to 12-month lookbacks, the most volatile earn nothing; weak t-statistics, no multiple-testing correction; no volatility spread in the whole market. Sources: Kaya and Mostowfi 2022 (to 2021-06); Liu, Tsyvinski and Wu 2022 (null, to 2018-12). Testable as a long-only trading claim.
- **Maximum daily return (lottery effect): sign disputed.** Null in the one source opened (-0.6 percent a week, t = -0.44); one abstract reports a negative effect above 1.5 percent a week, another a positive one. Source opened: Liu, Tsyvinski and Wu 2022 (to 2018-12). Testable as a predictive claim; the direction must be chosen before running.
- **Beta, skewness and kurtosis do not rank coins; downside beta carries a positive premium.** Beta spread -1.3 percent a week (t = -1.26), skewness and kurtosis near zero; downside-risk premium positive and significant in cross-sectional regressions over about 2,000 coins. Sources: Liu, Tsyvinski and Wu 2022 (to 2018-12); Dobrynskaya and Dubrovskiy 2022 (to 2020-11). Nulls testable as documented negatives; downside beta testable only in part.
- **Hourly bars: nothing found.** No cross-sectional study on hourly bars could be opened; an hourly version of a daily pattern is an extension of its source, to be stated as such.
- **Unverified leads** (abstract only or not opened; see the notes): Zaremba et al. 2021 (daily reversal in illiquid coins, daily momentum in the largest); Shen, Urquhart and Wang 2020 (reversal factor, to 2019-03); Dobrynskaya 2023 (momentum to about a month, reversal after, to 2020); Zhang and Li 2020 (idiosyncratic volatility positive); Li et al. 2021 (MAX positive); Grobys and Junttila 2021 (MAX negative); Jia, Liu and Yan 2021 (higher moments); Zhang et al. 2021 (downside risk positive); Burggraf and Rudolf 2021 (low volatility fails in the broad market); Wen et al. 2022 (intraday Bitcoin, to 2020-05); Bianchi and Dickerson (12-hour sorts); Han, Kang and Ryu (sample to 2023-08, overlaps dev: flagged).

## Derivatives

Notes: [derivatives.md](derivatives.md). Published evidence that perpetual-futures data predicts spot returns is thin: most work prices the contract or a hedged carry trade we cannot hold, and several key papers were readable only as abstracts.

- **The perpetual's premium over spot follows past returns and is large; the profit is in a hedged trade.** Mean absolute deviation 57 to 88 percent a year on five Binance coins, higher after the coin has risen, shrinking over time; no test of spot-return prediction. Source: He, Manela, Ross and von Wachter, working paper (sample to 2024-03, overlaps dev: flagged). Not testable as published (needs a short perpetual); it says a premium signal must be measured against the coin's own momentum.
- **Funding rate and price changes Granger-cause each other at 8 hours.** Both directions significant on BitMEX Bitcoin; no sign, size or costs reported. Source: Nimmagadda and Ammanamanchi 2019, not peer reviewed (sample to 2019-10). Testable as a predictive claim on hourly bars; the source does not fix the direction.
- **The Binance USDT perpetual is the main source of volatility for spot markets.** At five-minute realised volatility on Bitcoin a shock there raises expected volatility elsewhere by 3 to 4 percent; about volatility, not returns. Source: Alexander, Heck and Kaeck 2021 (sample 2021-01 to 2021-03). Testable only in part, as an hourly volatility forecast from the premium index.
- **Open interest and same-day volatility are negatively related on Binance and Bybit.** Contemporaneous regressions on daily Bitcoin data; not predictive, not about direction. Source: Chen, Ma and Nie 2024, working paper (sample to about 2023-09, overlaps dev: flagged). Testable only as a lagged variant the paper does not run; one year of train.
- **Data caveat: exchange-reported open interest cannot be reconciled with volume at short intervals.** For the Binance BTCUSDT perpetual the bound fails in up to 41 percent of hours in mid-2023. Source: Giagkiozis and Said 2024 (samples in 2023: flagged, a data-quality fact). Prefer daily levels and multi-day growth over hourly changes.
- **Practitioner null: funding changes move with prices and do not predict the next period.** Same-week R-squared 12.5 percent, next-week zero on BTC; a cross-sectional funding signal is said to look better before costs. Source: Presto Research 2024 (sample to about 2024: flagged; a lead, not evidence).
- **Unverified leads** (abstract only or not opened; see the notes): Schmeling, Schrimpf and Todorov, "Crypto carry" (a high futures premium predicts price crashes; the one claim about spot itself; sample end unknown: flagged); Chi et al. 2023 (basis the strongest cross-sectional predictor of crypto futures returns, daily only, 2017 to 2021); Lee, El Meslmani and Switzer 2020 (dated-futures basis a biased predictor of spot changes, to 2019-03); Shi 2022 (speculator and retail positions predict Bitcoin futures returns); Alexander et al. 2020 and Alexander and Heck 2020 (offshore perpetuals lead spot at one minute) against Baur and Dimpfl 2019 (spot leads regulated futures); Cheng et al. 2021 (longs liquidated about twice as often as shorts).
- **Nothing found:** no academic test of the funding rate, open-interest growth, long/short or top-trader ratios, taker imbalance or liquidations as predictors of spot returns on a multi-coin sample ending by 2022, and no hourly lead-lag study between perpetual and spot.

## Cross-market

Notes: [cross-market.md](cross-market.md). Access was poor in this run: only eight patterns rest on a source read in full, none of them on Bitcoin-to-altcoin lead-lag, and every numbered source is evidence on Bitcoin (one adds Ethereum). Every numbered source ends by 2022-12-21.

- **Exchange volume and Twitter opinion polarization precede Bitcoin returns by a day.** Positive one-day response, about 0.5 percent cumulative; combined long-short strategy Sharpe 1.77 against -0.77 for buy-and-hold in 2014, break-even cost about 25 bps per trade, profit on the short side in a falling year. Source: Garcia and Schweitzer 2015 (sample to 2014-12). Testable only in part: the volume half as a daily predictive claim; no free tweet history.
- **Search attention and price feed each other; attention leads in bubbles.** A 10 percent rise in Google searches is followed by about 2 percent over two weeks; Wikipedia views push the price up when it is above its 7-day average and down when below; no forecast test, no costs. Sources: Kristoufek 2013 (to 2013-06), Kristoufek 2015 (to 2014-02). Not testable as published: Google Trends is not point-in-time; a Wikipedia version only if the Data steward clears the series.
- **Inflation surprises move Bitcoin within minutes; no announcement premium.** -24 bps per standard deviation of CPI or PPI surprise in a 30-minute window (t = -2.44), nothing for growth news, mean announcement-window return zero. Source: Pinchuk, working paper (to 2021-05). Testable only in part: as a predicted null on hourly bars around CPI releases; needs a date file, and the reaction is over inside one hourly bar.
- **FOMC days: an unexpected rate rise lowers Bitcoin and Ethereum.** About -26 and -30 percent per percentage point of surprise on 43 days, not robust for Bitcoin; mean FOMC-day return 0.32 percent (Bitcoin) and 1.41 (Ethereum), not tested against other days. Source: Kyriazis et al. 2023, working paper (to 2022-12-21). Testable only in part: as a calendar claim on daily and hourly bars; needs a date file.
- **No stable link from gold or financial stress to Bitcoin (a null).** Coherence only in the 2013 Cypriot crisis. Source: Kristoufek 2015 (to 2014-02); the abstract of Liu and Tsyvinski adds no exposure to stocks, currencies or commodities (to 2018-05, tables not read). Testable here with the ETF bars as "session D predicts crypto day D+1", prior null.
- **On-chain usage leads price only at long horizons and fades; hash rate follows price.** Coherence at scales near 128 days, insignificant from 2013; transaction counts do not lead daily returns in a second source. Sources: Kristoufek 2015 (to 2014-02), Garcia and Schweitzer 2015 (to 2014-12). Testable only in part, Bitcoin only, prior null at our horizons.
- **Altcoin Twitter volume and sentiment at 3-hour sampling: weak out of sample.** Best training R-squared 0.24, test 0.03 to 0.11, best lag unstable; 71 days in 2017, mostly micro-caps, no costs. Source: Steinert and Herff 2018 (to 2017-06). Not testable; a prior against intraday sentiment signals.
- **Dispersion of coin returns shrinks in large market moves (herding), at hourly but not daily frequency.** Descriptive; no test of whether dispersion predicts returns. Source: Yarovaya, Matkovskyy and Jalan 2021 (to 2020-03). Supplies a state variable, not a published predictive claim.
- **Free point-in-time on-chain or sentiment series: none established.** Free and keyless but with revisions undocumented: the alternative.me Fear and Greed index (ingredients changed over time), Coin Metrics community files (non-commercial licence), DefiLlama stablecoin supply. Google Trends is not point-in-time by construction. See the table in the notes.
- **Unverified leads, Bitcoin-to-altcoin lead-lag** (abstracts only; no source opened in full): Guo, Sang, Tu and Wang 2024 (lagged returns of other coins predict a coin's return on Binance, long-short profit after costs; sample dates unknown: flagged); Wang and Ngene 2020 (Bitcoin forecasts other coins intraday, nonlinearly); Sifat, Mohamad and Shariff 2019 (a null: Bitcoin and Ethereum lead each other at hourly and daily frequency, not exploitable, to 2018-09); Bação et al. 2018 (a null: transmission is within the period, lagged feedback runs toward Bitcoin); Şoiman, Dumas and Jimenez-Garces 2022 (DeFi tokens exposed to the crypto market).
- **Unverified leads, other** (see the notes): Benigno and Rosa 2023 (Bitcoin unresponsive to macro and monetary news; sample dates unknown: flagged); Iyer 2022 (crypto-equity spillovers rose after 2020, same-period measures); Corbet et al. 2020 and Akyildirim et al. 2020 (volatility links to FOMC and VIX); Griffin and Shams 2020 against Kristoufek 2021 and 2022 (Tether flows precede Bitcoin rises, against issuance following prices); Ante, Fiedler and Strehle 2021 (stablecoin issuances); Hoang and Baur (exchange reserves); Cavalheiro et al. 2024 and Huang et al. 2024 (Fear and Greed index; sample dates unknown: flagged); one herding paper relating dispersion to returns by lagged market state (authors not confirmed).
- **Nothing found:** no published test of the previous US session predicting the next crypto day, of market breadth, average correlation or Bitcoin's volume share as a predictor, or of active addresses, MVRV or NVT out of sample. Weekend effects and crash contagion were not searched.
